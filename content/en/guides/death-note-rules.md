Death Note rests on an object and its instructions. The rules of the notebook, written in English on its first pages and revealed bit by bit over the chapters, matter as much as the characters. Light Yagami and L spend the whole series exploiting them, testing them and even making them up. Here are the ones you need to know.

## The basic rule

The first rule is the most famous: the person whose name is written in the notebook dies. Two conditions come with it straight away. The writer must have the victim's face in mind, which protects people who share the same name. And the name must be spelled correctly: a misspelled name has no effect.

These two constraints explain L's whole strategy. As long as Light knows neither his face nor his real name, the detective is out of reach, which lets him take the risk of showing himself at the university.

## Cause and circumstances

If a cause of death is written within 40 seconds of writing the name, it will happen. Otherwise, the victim dies of a heart attack, Kira's trademark. The writer then has six minutes and forty seconds to describe the circumstances.

The notebook can control a victim's actions before their death, up to 23 days in advance, but only within the limits of what is possible. You cannot impose a physically impossible action, or cause the death of anyone other than the people whose names are written. Light uses this rule to make criminals write messages before they die, while he is testing what the notebook can do.

## Ownership, memory and Shinigami

A Death Note that falls into the human world becomes the property of the first human who touches it. Its owner can see and hear the Shinigami, the god of death, who dropped it: Ryuk, in Light's case. Anyone who touches the notebook can also see that Shinigami.

Giving up ownership of the notebook erases every memory connected to it. This is the heart of Light's boldest plan: give up the notebook, deliberately lose his memory to appear innocent, then get it back by touching the notebook again at the right moment.

The Shinigami have their own rules. They extend their lives by killing humans, but a Shinigami who uses their notebook to save a human they love dies. Rem experiences this when she kills L and Watari to protect Misa.

## The eye deal

An owner can make a deal with their Shinigami: get its eyes in exchange for half of their remaining lifespan. These eyes let you see anyone's name and remaining lifespan just by looking at their face, which solves the name problem. Misa makes this deal twice, and Teru Mikami, Kira's successor, once.

## Light's fake rules

The most famous rules of the series are fake. To clear himself, Light has Ryuk write two invented rules at the back of the notebook. The first says that if the owner does not write a name for 13 days, they die. The second says that if the notebook is destroyed, everyone who has touched it dies.

The first one clears Light and Misa: they spent far more than 13 days in custody without writing a name, so they cannot be Kira. The second stops the investigators from destroying the notebook to test it. Near eventually concludes, rightly, that the 13-day rule is a fake.

## A few lesser-known rules

The notebook has no effect on children under 780 days old. If a name is misspelled by mistake four times, that person can no longer be killed by the notebook. Finally, a simple scrap of a page is enough: Light hides a piece of paper in his watch so he can kill discreetly, a trick that plays a part in the final showdown.

## The classic quiz traps

Questions about the rules often play on numbers: 40 seconds for the cause, six minutes and forty seconds for the circumstances, 23 days of control at most, 13 days for the fake rule. You also need to tell the real rules apart from Light's inventions, and remember that it is Rem, not Light, who writes L's name.

The “Rules” category of the Death Note quiz is one of the most demanding on the site: reading it once with these landmarks in mind makes a real difference.
