The type system is at the heart of every Pokémon battle. A move can deal double damage, half damage or even none at all, depending on the type of the move and the type of the Pokémon it hits. With 18 types, the full chart has 324 cells, which can be discouraging. The good news: a handful of logical patterns is enough to work out almost all of it.

## 18 types, added in three stages

The first games, Red and Green, had 15 types. The second generation, with Gold and Silver, added **Steel** and **Dark**, partly to balance the Psychic type, which was far too strong at the time. The sixth generation, with X and Y, added **Fairy**, to rebalance the Dragon type.

The 18 types are therefore: Normal, Fire, Water, Grass, Electric, Ice, Fighting, Poison, Ground, Flying, Psychic, Bug, Rock, Ghost, Dragon, Dark, Steel and Fairy.

## How damage is calculated

A move can be **super effective** (damage multiplied by 2), **neutral** (×1), **not very effective** (×0.5) or have **no effect** (×0). When a Pokémon has two types, the multipliers stack: an Ice move against a Dragon and Flying Pokémon, like Dragonite, deals four times the normal damage.

Another bonus matters a lot: a Pokémon using a move of its own type deals 50% more damage. So a Fire Pokémon using a Fire move hits harder than a Water Pokémon using the same move.

## The starter triangle

All the logic starts with the trio of starter Pokémon found in every generation: **Fire, Water, Grass**. Fire burns Grass, Water puts out Fire, Grass absorbs Water. This triangle is the first reflex to have, and extending it with common sense is enough to work out a good part of the chart.

Water is also super effective against Ground and Rock, which it erodes. Grass is super effective against Ground and Rock for the same reason, and Fire against Ice, Bug and Steel, which it melts or burns.

## The logical pairings

Many relationships can be explained by how we picture the elements:

- **Electric** is super effective against **Water** (water conducts electricity) and **Flying** (lightning strikes whatever is in the air).
- **Ice** is super effective against **Flying**, **Grass**, **Ground** and **Dragon**, creatures that cope badly with the cold.
- **Rock** is super effective against **Flying**, **Fire**, **Ice** and **Bug**.
- **Fighting** breaks **Rock**, **Ice**, **Steel**, **Normal** and **Dark**, but it is weak against **Psychic**, **Flying** and **Fairy**.
- **Ground** is super effective against **Fire**, **Electric**, **Poison**, **Rock** and **Steel**.

## The immunities you must know

The zero cells matter most in battle, because a move with no effect wastes a whole turn. There are eight of them:

- **Normal** and **Fighting** have no effect on **Ghost**.
- **Ghost** has no effect on **Normal**.
- **Electric** has no effect on **Ground**.
- **Ground** has no effect on **Flying**.
- **Psychic** has no effect on **Dark**.
- **Poison** has no effect on **Steel**.
- **Dragon** has no effect on **Fairy**.

Some Abilities add other immunities. The best known is Levitate, which makes a Pokémon immune to Ground moves even if it is not a Flying type.

## The special cases

**Dragon** is super effective against itself, which makes duels between dragons especially brutal. **Ghost** is in the same situation: it is super effective against Ghost. They are the only two types in this case.

**Steel** is the defensive type par excellence: it resists ten types and is only weak to Fire, Fighting and Ground. **Fairy**'s role is to counter Dragon and Fighting, but it fears Steel and Poison.

**Normal** is not super effective against any type. Its strength lies in the same-type bonus and in the power of some of its moves.

## A method to remember the chart

Rather than learning all 324 cells, go step by step. Start with the Fire, Water, Grass triangle. Add the eight immunities, which are the most costly mistakes. Then learn the weaknesses of your favorite Pokémon: knowing the weak points of the types you play often is worth more than a whole chart learned by heart and then forgotten.
