KamiQuiz has several thousand questions, and a single wrong one is enough to spoil a game. Here is how they are written, sorted and corrected, so you know what you can rely on when you play.

## Where the questions come from

Every quiz starts from the work itself: the manga chapters, the episodes of the series, the films, the games. For the general knowledge, history and geography quizzes, the questions rely on established, verifiable facts, such as dates, capitals or official records.

We deliberately avoid three kinds of questions. Questions about things that change often, like a sales ranking or a season still airing, quickly become wrong. Questions based on an opinion, like “the best character”, have no right answer. And questions that depend on a precise translation are worded carefully, because the same term can change from one edition to another.

KamiQuiz was first written in French. The English version of each quiz is translated from the French original, using the official English names of characters, places and techniques.

## An explanation for every answer

Every question comes with an explanation, shown right after the answer. It is the most important rule of the site. A good explanation does not just repeat the right answer: it gives the context, the episode or the detail that helps you remember it. It is also a safeguard against mistakes, because an answer that cannot be justified in one or two sentences is often a badly asked question.

## Four difficulty levels

Every question is sorted into one of four levels:

- **Easy**: what anyone who has watched or read the work knows, like the name of the hero or their team.
- **Medium**: what an attentive viewer remembers, like the name of a technique or an important place.
- **Hard**: the details of side arcs, the precise rules of a power system, less prominent characters.
- **Expert**: details only devoted fans know, like a chapter number or a rarely mentioned rule.

These levels drive several game modes. Ascension mode starts with easy questions and ends with the hardest ones, and Expert mode only keeps the last two levels.

## The art of wrong answers

In a multiple-choice quiz, the wrong answers matter as much as the right one. An obviously wrong answer makes the question useless. A wrong answer too close to the right one makes it unfair. We look for plausible options: characters from the same universe, techniques that exist but do not fit, nearby dates. The goal is for someone who knows the work to find the right answer, and for someone who guesses to really have a one-in-four chance.

The order of the answers is shuffled in every game, so you cannot memorize the position rather than the answer.

## Cleaning up the question bank

A question bank wears out over time: duplicates, inconsistent categories, terms that change. We clean it up regularly. During the last overhaul of the site, this work removed 19 duplicate questions, deleted questions whose premise was wrong, fixed a question that accepted two right answers, and harmonized the terminology, for example by using the official terms of EA Sports FC, such as SBC (Squad Building Challenges) or TOTW (Team of the Week).

## When an error slips through

Despite this care, an error can get through, especially on series that are still ongoing, where a reveal can change the meaning of an answer. If you spot one, write to us at contact@kamiquiz.com with the quiz, the beginning of the question and, if possible, a source: a chapter, an episode or an official page. We fix the question, or remove it if it cannot be saved.

## Why all this matters

A quiz is only useful if you can trust it. That is what makes it possible to learn while playing, and that is why every universe also has a review page where all the questions are visible with their answers and explanations. If a question seems doubtful to you, the review page is the best place to find it and report it to us.
