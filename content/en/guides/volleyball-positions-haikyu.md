Haikyu!! has made many readers curious about volleyball, and that is largely thanks to how precisely Haruichi Furudate shows the role of each player. A match is not just a series of spectacular spikes: every position has its mission, its constraints and its heroes. Here is how a team works, with the characters of the series as examples.

## Six players, three touches

A team has six players on the court. It is allowed three touches to send the ball back, almost always in the same order: a receive, a set, an attack. A touch by a player blocking at the net does not count toward that total. A set is played to 25 points with a two-point lead, and the deciding set to 15 points.

Players rotate from one position to the next each time their team wins back the serve. Three of them are in the front row, near the net, and three in the back row. This rotation forces everyone to defend and attack in turn, and it is why a coach thinks so carefully about the order of the players.

## The setter, the conductor

The setter touches almost every second ball. Their job is to choose who will attack, where and at what tempo, while hiding their intentions from the opposing block. It is the most cerebral position on the court, and Haikyu!! devotes three of its most memorable characters to it.

**Tobio Kageyama** embodies precision: his sets arrive to the millimeter, to the point of feeding Hinata with his eyes closed. **Toru Oikawa**, the captain of Aoba Johsai, does not have Kageyama's natural talent, but he knows how to get the best out of every hitter, a quality the series presents as the mark of great setters. **Kenma Kozume**, at Nekoma, plays the way you play a video game: he observes, tests, then traps his opponents. Kageyama's path, nicknamed the “King of the Court” in middle school because no one could keep up with his demands, also shows the other side of the position: a setter has to adapt to their teammates, not the other way around.

## The middle blocker, at the heart of the net

Middle blockers stand in the middle of the net. They have two missions: blocking the opponents' attacks, moving from one end of the net to the other, and hitting the fastest attacks, set just above the tape. It is traditionally the position of the tallest players.

That is what makes **Shoyo Hinata** so unusual. Too short for the position on paper, he makes up for it with his jump and his speed. His coach, Keishin Ukai, also gives him the role of **decoy**: by jumping on every play, he draws the opposing block and frees up his teammates. **Kei Tsukishima** represents the other face of the middle blocker. Rather than going for the point at all costs, he reads the opponents' play, slows the ball down with his blocks and sets up his defense's work. His block on Ushijima, in the prefectural final, is one of the high points of the series. At Nekoma, the captain **Tetsuro Kuroo** is also a middle blocker, and the most cunning blocker in Tokyo.

## The outside hitter, the all-rounder

Outside hitters, also called wing spikers, play on the wings. They have to both receive the opponents' serves and hit in attack. Because they take part in almost every play, they are often the most complete players on a team.

At Karasuno, **Daichi Sawamura** is the backbone of the receive: quiet in attack, he steadies the whole team. **Ryunosuke Tanaka** brings his energy and very tight cross-court spikes. **Asahi Azumane** is the ace, the player who gets the ball when everything is going wrong. Among the opponents, **Kotaro Bokuto** of Fukurodani and **Wakatoshi Ushijima** of Shiratorizawa are hitters of this kind, able to score against any block.

## The opposite, the pure attacker

The opposite stands across from the setter in the rotation. They receive little and focus almost entirely on attacking, including from the back row. In professional volleyball, the opposite is often the team's top scorer. Haikyu!! puts this position less in the spotlight than the others, because Karasuno builds its game around its wing spikers and Hinata and Kageyama's quick attack.

## The libero, the defensive specialist

The libero wears a jersey of a different color so they can be spotted immediately. They are a specialist in defense and receiving. They replace a back-row player, often a middle blocker, and can come on and off as many times as needed. In exchange, their rules are strict: they only play in the back row, do not block and cannot attack a ball that is above the net.

**Yu Nishinoya**, nicknamed Karasuno's Guardian Deity, is the most famous libero in the series, with his acrobatic digs. Nekoma's libero, **Morisuke Yaku**, illustrates a whole team built on defense: the cats let almost no ball drop.

## The pinch server

Volleyball allows substitutions at any time, which means a player can come on just to serve. **Tadashi Yamaguchi** plays this role at Karasuno with his float serve, hit without spin so that the ball changes direction at the last moment. His arc is one of the most telling in the series: coming on cold, for a single serve, at a decisive moment, takes immense composure.

## The classic quiz traps

Hinata is a middle blocker, not an outside hitter, despite his height. The libero can neither block nor attack above the net, which sets them apart from an ordinary defender. A block touch does not count among the team's three touches. Finally, Kenma and Oikawa are both setters, but Kuroo is not: he plays middle blocker alongside Kenma.

With these landmarks, the “Volleyball” category of the Haikyu!! quiz becomes much easier, and you will watch the matches of the series with new eyes.
