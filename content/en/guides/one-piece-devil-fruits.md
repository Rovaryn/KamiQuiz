In One Piece, almost every major fight revolves around an object that looks harmless: a fruit whose skin is covered in swirls. Eating it grants a superhuman power, but takes away the ability to swim forever. This bargain, which Eiichiro Oda set up in the very first chapter with Luffy, shapes the whole world of the series. Here is what you need to understand how these fruits work, and to stop getting caught out by the quiz questions.

## A power and a curse

Each Devil Fruit holds a unique power. Only one copy of each fruit exists at any given time: when its user dies, the power reappears in an ordinary fruit somewhere in the world. That is why Ace's Flame-Flame Fruit (Mera Mera no Mi) could be offered as a prize at the Dressrosa Colosseum, where Sabo finally obtained it.

The price is always the same. A user sinks like a stone in the sea and loses their strength as soon as they are immersed, even partially. Seastone, a stone that gives off the same energy as the sea, has the same effect: that is why Marine handcuffs and the cells of Impel Down are made of it. A user also cannot eat a second fruit: the common rule is that their body cannot take it. Blackbeard is the only known exception, and his case remains one of the great mysteries of the series.

Finally, a Devil Fruit is anything but appetizing. Every character who has eaten one describes a horrible taste, which does not stop some of them from swallowing one without knowing what they are doing, like Luffy as a child.

## Paramecia: the catch-all type

The vast majority of known fruits belong to the Paramecia type. What they have in common is negative: they turn their user neither into an animal nor into an element. Beyond that, anything goes. Some change the user's body, like Buggy's fruit, which lets him split into pieces. Others act on the surroundings, like Trafalgar Law's fruit, which creates a “Room” inside which he rearranges everything. Others still produce a substance, like Mr. 3's wax or Charlotte Cracker's biscuits.

This variety explains why Paramecia are so hard to classify, and why several users of this type are among the most dangerous people in the world: Big Mom and her power over souls, Whitebeard and his earthquakes, Doflamingo and his strings.

## Zoan: turning into an animal

Zoan fruits let their user turn into an animal, completely or partly. A user usually has three forms: human, animal and hybrid. Chopper, who ate the Human-Human Fruit while he was a reindeer, is a special case: he developed many in-between forms thanks to his Rumble Balls.

Zoan fruits also boost stamina and recovery. Two subtypes are rarer and more powerful than average. Ancient Zoan fruits turn their users into prehistoric animals, like the dinosaurs of Kaido's crew. Mythical Zoan fruits turn them into legendary creatures: Marco's phoenix, which gives him healing flames, or Kaido's Eastern dragon.

The most famous reveal about this type concerns Luffy himself. The fruit he thought was the Gum-Gum Fruit (Gomu Gomu no Mi), a Paramecia, is in fact a Mythical Zoan, the Human-Human Fruit, Model: Nika (Hito Hito no Mi). The World Government had hidden its real name for centuries.

## Logia: becoming an element

Logia fruits are the rarest. They let their user turn into a natural element, produce it and control it: fire for Ace and later Sabo, ice for Aokiji, magma for Akainu, light for Kizaru, lightning for Enel, sand for Crocodile.

Their main advantage is defensive. A Logia user can let blows pass through their elemental body, which makes them practically untouchable for an ordinary opponent. Throughout the first part of the series, these were the fruits that seemed invincible.

There are two answers to them. The first is the opposite element: Luffy, immune to electricity, could hit Enel, and water hardens Crocodile's sand. The second is Armament Haki, which lets a fighter touch a Logia user's real body. After the two-year timeskip, Haki becomes the key to almost every high-level fight, and the Logia advantage fades against experienced fighters.

## Awakening: when the power overflows

A user who pushes their fruit to the limit can awaken it. For Paramecia, awakening extends the power to the surroundings: Doflamingo turns the buildings of Dressrosa into strings, Katakuri turns the ground into mochi. For Zoan, awakening mostly multiplies toughness and ferocity, at the risk of losing self-control, like the awakened Zoan users of Impel Down. The details of Logia awakening remain vaguer in the manga.

The awakening of Luffy's fruit, Gear 5, is a special case: it gives his body and everything around him the properties of rubber, with a deliberately cartoonish tone that reflects the nature of the sun god Nika.

## The classic quiz traps

A few mix-ups come up again and again in the One Piece quiz. A fruit's type does not depend on its power: a Paramecia can be far more dangerous than a Logia. Luffy's fruit has no longer been a Paramecia since the Wano reveal. Smoker, with his smoke, is indeed a Logia, while Mr. 3 and his wax belong to the Paramecia, because he produces a substance without becoming one. Finally, the Japanese name of a fruit almost always repeats the same word twice, followed by “no Mi”: Mera Mera, Hie Hie, Gura Gura. It is a good way to spot the wrong answers.

Once you have these basics in mind, all that is left is to put them to the test. Questions about powers make up a good part of the One Piece quiz, and they range from the best-known fruits to the minor users of the latest arcs.
